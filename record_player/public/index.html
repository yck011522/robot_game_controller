YOUR FIRST EXPLORATION

What makes the data useful?

Start with a normal game, a short game, and one with a large A/B difference. These are observations to discuss, not automatic player ratings.

  1. Orient yourself. Are P1–P6 left-to-right for both cameras? Try full frame and the lower-area crop. Which makes hands and bodies easier to read?
  2. Follow one player. Click a dial card and scrub its traces. Can you distinguish a small steady lead from continued turning while the robot is limited?
  3. Compare speeds. Does the joint-equivalent dial speed make sense alongside robot speed? Do you need raw dial speed or torque visible more often?
  4. Check alignment. Find a clear hand movement, robot response, and loudness event. Does audio need an offset? Relative audio starts and cross-device clocks are not verified.
  5. Inspect the scene. Orbit and zoom. Which environment pieces obstruct the view? Are the bucket tool and scoring buckets legible?
  6. Try controller guides. Suggest positions, then drag them with a populated frame visible. Do they stay close to held controllers across several games?
  7. Evaluate the browser. Do duration, travel, lead P95, and limited time help you find contrasting games? Which flags are misleading?

When sharing feedback, note the recording ID, timestamp, team/player, and what you expected to see. Short games, large lead, and loudness alone do not establish player skill or intent.
